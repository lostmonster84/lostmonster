## What This Covers

Copy and content strategy for single-purpose landing pages: waitlist signups, product launches, feature announcements, campaign pages, event registrations. Writing the words that make the page convert -- not layout (that's DEMX) or visual quality (that's SOFAX).

**This supplement is universal.** It must NEVER reference a specific project, brand, colour, or client. It teaches the craft -- the patterns that make this job type work regardless of who it's for. The project's design guide handles brand identity. This handles quality.

## Patterns (What the Best All Do)

### Pattern 1: Outcome Headlines (Not Feature Headlines)
**What:** The headline states what the user GETS, not what the product IS. "Build in a weekend, Scale to millions" (outcome) vs "Serverless Postgres Database" (feature). Outcome headlines answer "What happens for ME?"
**Applicability:** Universal
**Evidence:** #4 Supabase ("Build in a weekend, Scale to millions"), #8 Superhuman ("Superpowers, everywhere you work"), #2 Notion ("Meet your 24/7 AI team"), #9 Raycast ("Your shortcut to everything")
**How to apply:** Write the feature headline first. Then ask "So what? What does the user GET from this?" Rewrite as the outcome. Test: does the headline make sense if the user has never heard of the product category? If yes, it's an outcome headline.

### Pattern 2: The Number Anchor
**What:** At least one specific number in the hero area. Can be: time saved ("47 seconds"), cost comparison ("$687 vs $55,574"), ROI ("67%"), scale ("150,000+ daily"), or speed ("30 seconds to start"). Numbers create concreteness and trust.
**Applicability:** Universal
**Evidence:** #11 Amie ("47 seconds"), #3 Better Stack ("30x cheaper", "$687/mo"), #12 Deel ("67% ROI"), #7 Neon ("150,000+ endpoints"), #13 Betterstack ("30 seconds")
**How to apply:** Identify the single most impressive metric about the product or offer. Place it in the hero -- either in the headline, subheadline, or as a standalone stat. Format: bold, large, optionally with a contrasting color. If no real metric exists, use a comparative number ("2 fields vs 11 fields", "3 steps vs 15 steps").

### Pattern 3: Subheadline Explains Mechanism
**What:** The headline hooks with the outcome. The subheadline explains HOW -- the mechanism. "Infinite minds, built for teamwork. Answer questions, prioritize tasks, and write reports -- all while you sleep." The subheadline turns the promise into something believable.
**Applicability:** Universal
**Evidence:** #2 Notion ("Infinite minds, built for teamwork. Answer questions, prioritize tasks, and write reports"), #4 Supabase (Postgres + Auth + APIs + Edge Functions), #5 Cal.com ("fully customizable scheduling software for individuals, businesses, and developers"), #9 Raycast ("A collection of powerful productivity tools all within an extendable launcher")
**How to apply:** Headline = outcome (5-12 words). Subheadline = mechanism (15-25 words). The subheadline should answer: "How does this work?" or "What specifically does it do?" Use concrete nouns and verbs, not abstract concepts.

### Pattern 4: CTA Text as Micro-Copy
**What:** CTA button text is 2-4 words that name the action AND reduce friction simultaneously. "Start building for free" = action (start building) + friction reducer (for free). "Get started in 30 seconds" = action (get started) + time commitment (30 seconds).
**Applicability:** Universal
**Evidence:** #6 Clerk ("Start building for free"), #3 Better Stack ("Start for free"), #1 Vercel Ship ("Notify me"), #5 Cal.com ("Sign up with Google"), #2 Notion ("Try for free")
**How to apply:** Formula: [Action verb] + [object or qualifier]. Acceptable patterns: "Start for free", "Join the waitlist", "Get early access", "Try for free", "Download for Mac". Avoid: generic "Submit", "Click here", "Learn more" (for primary CTAs). The CTA text should tell the user exactly what will happen next.

### Pattern 5: Friction Reducer Copy (Adjacent to CTA)
**What:** A single line (5-8 words) positioned directly below or beside the CTA button that addresses the primary objection. This is NOT the subheadline -- it's a separate micro-copy element at the point of action.
**Applicability:** Universal
**Evidence:** #14 Woodpecker ("No credit card required"), #13 Betterstack ("Get started in 30 seconds"), #7 Neon (free tier messaging), #3 Better Stack (free + no credit card)
**How to apply:** Identify the #1 objection for this page type. Write a 5-8 word answer. Place it within 16px of the CTA button. Common patterns: Waitlist = "We'll only email you once." Signup = "No credit card required." Download = "Free, no account needed." Event = "Save your spot -- no commitment." Demo = "Pick a time that works for you."

### Pattern 6: Social Proof Copy (Names + Specifics + Outcomes)
**What:** Testimonials include the person's full name, title, company, and a specific outcome. "If it's something that's repetitive, we have a Notion Agent for it." -- Ben Levick, Head of AI & Operations, Ramp. This is infinitely more persuasive than "Great product! -- J."
**Applicability:** Universal
**Evidence:** #2 Notion (Ben Levick/Ramp, Morgane Palomares/Braintrust), #5 Cal.com (Kent C. Dodds, Guillermo Rauch, Flo Merian), #10 Raycast (Guillermo Rauch/Vercel, Marques Brownlee, Wes Bos), #3 Better Stack (named developers with real tweets)
**How to apply:** Each testimonial MUST include: (1) a specific claim or outcome (not just "love it"), (2) person's full name, (3) title and company. Ideal length: 1-2 sentences. The quote should describe what changed, what they achieved, or what surprised them -- not just that they like the product.

### Pattern 7: Section Headlines Follow Narrative Arc
**What:** As the user scrolls, section headlines form a story: Problem > Solution > How it works > Proof > Action. Each headline should make sense even if the user skips straight to it. Headlines are the "speed-reading path."
**Applicability:** Universal
**Evidence:** #2 Notion AI (AI team > Agents > Search > Meeting Notes > Use cases > FAQ -- progressive depth), #8 Supabase (Build > Scale > Trusted > Templates > Community), #5 Cal.com (Better scheduling > Trusted > How it works > Features > Love)
**How to apply:** Write all section headlines in sequence. Read them top-to-bottom. Do they tell a story? Can someone who reads ONLY headlines understand the page? If reading just headlines feels disjointed, rewrite them as a narrative sequence.

### Pattern 8: Sentence Length and Reading Level
**What:** Landing page copy uses short sentences (under 15-20 words). Reading level should be accessible (8th grade or below). Complex ideas are broken into simple, concrete statements. Passive voice is rare. Every sentence earns its place.
**Applicability:** Universal
**Evidence:** Research data: sentences exceeding 15-20 words reduce cognitive fluency and trust. #4 Supabase (7-word headline), #8 Superhuman (4-word headline), #9 Raycast (5-word headline + 16-word subheadline)
**How to apply:** After writing, audit every sentence. If over 20 words, split it. If passive voice, rewrite as active. If it uses jargon, replace with plain language. If removing a sentence doesn't weaken the section, remove it. The best landing page copy feels effortless to read.

## Anti-Patterns (What to Avoid)

| # | Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | Example |
|---|-------------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | Feature-list headlines | "AI-Powered Real-Time Collaboration Platform" describes the product but gives the user no reason to care. Features belong in the subheadline or body, not the headline | Headlines that read like product specifications |
| 2 | Vague superlatives | "The best", "world-class", "cutting-edge", "revolutionary" -- empty words that every competitor also claims. Replace with specific evidence | "The world's best project management tool" (vs "47 seconds to summarize meetings") |
| 3 | "We" copy (company-centered) | "We built the fastest database" centers the company. "Your database in 30 seconds" centers the user. Landing pages are about the visitor, not the maker | Multiple paragraphs starting with "We" or "Our" |
| 4 | Wall-of-text sections | Any section with more than 3-4 sentences of body copy without a visual break (heading, bullet, image) loses readers. Landing pages are not blog posts | Feature descriptions that run 100+ words in a single paragraph |
| 5 | Generic CTA text | "Submit", "Click here", "Learn more" (as primary CTA). These tell the user nothing about what happens next. CTA text should name the action | Primary CTA that says "Submit" or "Get started" without context |
| 6 | AI-flavoured punctuation and phrasing | Em dashes where commas or periods work, "streamline", "leverage", "utilize", "cutting-edge", "harness the power of". These signal AI-generated copy and erode trust | Copy that reads like it came from a prompt: polished but lifeless |
| 7 | Testimonials without attribution | "This product changed my workflow" -- J. Who is J? What company? What specifically changed? Unattributed quotes are worthless for trust building | Anonymous quotes, first-name-only attribution, no company context |

## Benchmarks

### Word Count Targets by Page Type
| Page Type | Hero (headline + subheadline) | Total Body Copy | Section Headlines (all) |
|-----------|-------------------------------|-----------------|------------------------|
| Waitlist / event | 15-30 words | 200-400 words | 3-5 headlines |
| Product signup | 20-40 words | 800-1,200 words | 5-8 headlines |
| Feature launch | 25-45 words | 1,200-2,100 words | 8-12 headlines |
| Competitor comparison | 20-40 words | 1,000-1,800 words | 6-10 headlines |

### Copy Element Benchmarks
| Element | Length | Example |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Hero headline | 5-12 words | "Build in a weekend, Scale to millions" (7 words) |
| Hero subheadline | 15-25 words | "A fully customizable scheduling software for individuals, businesses taking calls and developers building scheduling platforms" (Cal.com, 15 words) |
| CTA button text | 2-4 words | "Start for free", "Join waitlist", "Notify me" |
| Friction reducer | 5-8 words | "No credit card required", "Get started in 30 seconds" |
| Testimonial quote | 15-35 words (1-2 sentences) | "If it's something that's repetitive, we have a Notion Agent for it." (13 words) |
| Section headline | 4-10 words | "Trusted by fast-growing companies" (5 words) |
| Feature description | 20-50 words per feature | Short paragraph or 2-3 bullet points |
| FAQ answer | 30-80 words per answer | Concise, direct, no marketing fluff |

### Readability Targets
| Metric | Target |
|--------|--------|
| Average sentence length | Under 15 words |
| Reading level | 8th grade or below (Flesch-Kincaid) |
| Passive voice frequency | Under 10% of sentences |
| Jargon density | Maximum 1 technical term per paragraph (with context) |
| "We/Our" vs "You/Your" ratio | "You" should outnumber "we" 3:1 or better |

## Mobile Patterns

### Mobile Copy Adjustments
- Hero headline must not exceed 3 lines at 390px width (28-36px font). If it wraps beyond 3 lines, the headline is too long -- shorten it for mobile or use responsive text
- Subheadline: consider hiding or shortening on mobile if it pushes CTA below fold
- CTA text: same on mobile as desktop (consistency), but friction reducer may need repositioning (below button, not beside)
- Testimonials: shorter quotes work better on mobile. If a testimonial exceeds 2 lines on mobile, consider showing only the key sentence
- FAQ answers: collapse behind accordion on mobile. Show only the question until tapped
- Section headlines: must be readable in 1-2 lines at 390px. If a headline wraps to 3+ lines on mobile, rewrite shorter

### Copy Hierarchy on Mobile
The scroll order creates a reading priority. On mobile, the first 844px of content is everything. The mobile copy priority stack:
1. Headline (must communicate value alone)
2. CTA (must be visible and actionable)
3. Friction reducer (must be adjacent to CTA)
4. Subheadline (expandable detail)
5. Social proof (first layer -- logos)
6. Everything else (scrollable)

## Accessibility Patterns

### Copy-Specific A11y
- Image alt text: describe what the image SHOWS, not what it IS. "Dashboard showing 47% increase in meeting efficiency" not "product screenshot"
- Link text: descriptive, not "click here" or "learn more". "Read the case study" or "View pricing plans"
- Error messages: specific, helpful, and linked to the field. "Please enter a valid email address" not "Invalid input"
- Form labels: visible (not placeholder-only). Label text above the input, not disappearing inside it
- CTA button text: must make sense out of context (screen reader users navigate by buttons). "Start your free trial" not "Start"
- Heading hierarchy: one `<h1>` (hero headline), sequential `<h2>`s for sections, `<h3>`s within sections. Never skip levels
- Language attribute: `lang="en"` on `<html>` element for screen reader pronunciation

### Error Message Copy Patterns
- Be specific: "Password must have at least 8 characters, including one uppercase letter and one number" not "Invalid password"
- Be helpful: suggest the fix, don't just state the problem
- Be human: "That email doesn't look right -- check for typos?" not "Error: Invalid email format"
- Place at field level (inline) AND provide a summary at form top for multiple errors

## Performance Patterns

### Copy Decisions That Affect Performance
- Fewer words = faster render. A 2,100-word page loads and renders slower than a 400-word page. Match word count to page type (see benchmarks above)
- System fonts for body copy eliminate font loading delay. Reserve custom fonts for headlines (1-2 weights only)
- Avoid dynamic copy (JS-rendered personalized text) above the fold unless SSR'd. Dynamic text that requires JS to render delays LCP
- FAQ sections: use native `<details>/<summary>` for accordion rather than JS-powered components. Lighter, faster, accessible by default

## Planning Implications

See CODAX supplement for full planning guidance. Key points for WORDX:

- Copy should be written BEFORE design. The story drives the layout, not the other way around. WORDX delivers copy, then DEMX builds around it
- Landing page copy is NOT website copy. It serves one goal. Every sentence must move the reader toward the CTA. If a sentence doesn't serve conversion, cut it
- Word count varies dramatically by page type: a waitlist page needs 200-400 words total, a feature launch needs 1,200-2,100 words. Scope copy accordingly
- Social proof copy requires real data: real names, real companies, real quotes. If this data doesn't exist yet, flag it as a dependency -- don't ship with placeholder quotes
- The hero is 70% of the copy effort even though it's 10% of the word count. Getting the headline right is the hardest part

- [ ] At least one specific number in hero area -- **Verify:** scan hero for numeric claim

- [ ] Friction reducer within 16px of each CTA -- **Verify:** text element adjacent to button addressing primary objection
